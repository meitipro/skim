---
title: Good questions
description: How to write a card whose questions can be answered in a specific way.
---

A card has 3 to 5 questions of at most 120 characters each. They are plain sentences, public on the card, and the same for every proposal on it.

## What makes a question answerable

A question is good when a skimming reader could repeat the answer. The judge counts an answer as FOUND when the opening states **a number, a date, a named action or a stated consequence**. Anything vague, hinted or pointing to something later is MISSING.

| A question | A FOUND answer | A MISSING answer |
|---|---|---|
| What is being asked of the DAO | "We ask for 40,000 USDC to rewrite the docs" | "We would like support" |
| How much it costs | "40,000 USDC, paid in two halves" | "A modest budget" |
| The date a decision is needed | "By December 5" | "Soon", "as discussed" |
| What happens if it does not pass | "The docs stay as they are" | Not stated |

## Loose questions show

A DAO that writes questions any text answers makes a weak card. The questions are public, so that is visible, and the site's card form shows a quick preview of a sample proposal before the card becomes the rule.

## Keep it to what voters need

Three to five is the range for a reason: the window is 80 to 200 words, and every question has to fit in it. A typical answer needs about 20 words.
