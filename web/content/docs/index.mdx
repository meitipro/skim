Skim lets a DAO write the questions every proposal must answer in its opening. An author submits a proposal with a small fee, the contract cuts it to the first 120 words in code, GenLayer validators read only that window and label each question FOUND or MISSING, and a proposal opens for voting only when the top of the page answers all of them.

<Callout title="The question validators answer">
  Could a reader who stops here say how much it costs, and what is being asked?
</Callout>

## Who it is for

- **Voters**, who see the ask, the cost and the date in the first screen, so a skim is enough to decide whether to read on.
- **Authors**, who get a fast, neutral check before posting, with the missing answers named, not a reviewer's mood.
- **DAOs and committees**, who get a bar for clarity that every proposal meets, with no one deciding what a good proposal is.

## One proposal, start to finish

1. The DAO opens a **card**: 3 to 5 questions, a window of 80 to 200 words, a fee of 0 to 10 GEN.
2. An author **submits** a proposal and pays the fee. The contract cuts it to its window, title included.
3. Validators **read** only that window and label each question FOUND or MISSING.
4. The contract applies the verdict in code: **SKIMMABLE** opens it and returns the fee, **REWRITE** names the missing count, **SHELVED** follows a third failure, and the owner can **waive** the rule.
5. `withdraw` is the only call that pays.

## What Skim is not

- Not a judge of the proposal. It never says a proposal is good, only that the top of the page answers the questions.
- Not a style police. Length, tone and language do not matter, only whether each question has an answer.
- Not a gate on ideas. An owner can waive the rule, and a skimmable proposal can still lose the vote.
- Not a reader of the full text. Only the window is read, by design.
